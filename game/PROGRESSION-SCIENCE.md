# Mission progression and science boundaries

New Mars missions begin with stored air, prepared meals, packaged rations and a finite 36 kWh emergency reserve. At most 12 kWh is available per training sol until permanent base power is connected. Read the console and locate the crew shelter before constructing power. Older saves skip these introductory checks and retain existing progress.

Sequence: safety checks → array and battery → base wiring → commission communications → extract water → purify and check → request pre-positioned seeds → collect marked cargo → plant, water, harvest and prepare food → rover charger → supported soil shielding → culture supplement → exploration.

Water extraction accumulates untreated water separately. Purification and a simulated quality check are required before transfer to the clean tank. This is an educational abstraction, not a water-treatment procedure. Crops require prepared growing media, nutrients, light and controlled air; they supplement packaged meals.

Cargo is pre-positioned near Mars. Earth authorizes requests by radio; a three-training-sol delivery is not an Earth-to-Mars flight. Actual interplanetary transport takes months. Growth, airlock cycles, suit endurance and supply timing are accelerated or tuned gameplay, not operational durations. The Moon five-minute lighting cycle is also a training compression.

Physical shielding remains when power fails. Shelter power supports equipment; it is not an active radiation shield. Soil upgrades add up to three half-metre courses behind the crew shelter. Tint elsewhere is cosmetic. This partial visual representation and its tuned dose reductions are not a validated structural or radiation design.
